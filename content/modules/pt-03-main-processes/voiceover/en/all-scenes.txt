Feed production has six main processes. To maintain the equipment and troubleshoot problems, you need to know what each one does.

One: intake and cleaning, the first line of defense. Trucks are unloaded, and foreign objects like stones, metal and string are removed. Dust and fines are separated, and material is sent to the right storage.

Two: dosing and weighing. Certified scales deliver exact quantities of macro and micro ingredients, within the tolerances of the formulation.

Three: size reduction, or grinding. Hammer mills bring materials to the required particle size, which matters for mixing, pellet quality and digestibility. For maintenance, remember: performance depends on hammer condition, sieve integrity and proper air balance. Inspect hammer wear and sieves regularly.

Four: mixing. Dry and liquid components are blended into a homogeneous formula, so that every portion of feed has the intended nutrition.

Five: compacting, in three stages. Conditioning adds steam or liquids for the right moisture and temperature. This improves pellet durability and reduces pathogens. Pelleting forces the mash through a die; die and roller specifications set pellet diameter, length and quality. Cooling brings pellets to within five degrees of ambient temperature and dries them for safe storage. Compacting does two jobs: it sanitizes through heat, and it makes a durable product.

Six: finished products. Feed is bagged at set weights, or loaded onto trucks in bulk for dispatch.

Intake, dosing, grinding, mixing, compacting, and finished products: six processes, one chain. Now take the quiz.
